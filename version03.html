How the week runs

Every day follows the same shape: a short explanation, then hands on hardware for the rest of the session. Nothing we teach stays theoretical past twenty minutes.

Two tracks, one room

Children work side by side but on kit matched to their age, so nobody is bored and nobody is lost.

Foundation

Ages 8–11 · BBC micro:bit

Block-based coding on a board built for young hands. Immediate feedback — press a button, something lights up.

  • Sensors: light, temperature, motion, sound
  • Their first working input–output loop
  • A smart-home gadget they design themselves
  • Presenting their build to the room

Advanced

Ages 12–16 · Keyestudio / Arduino

Real wiring, real components, text-based code. They assemble a four-wheel mecanum robot car with ultrasonic sensors, program it to sense and steer for itself, and debug it when it doesn't.

  • Reading and writing real Arduino code
  • Breadboards, motors, servos and ultrasonic sensors
  • A mecanum robot car that navigates on its own
  • Explaining a design decision under questioning

Questions parents ask

Does my child need to know how to code?

No. Most children arrive having never written a line. The Foundation track starts from nothing, and the Advanced track starts from logic rather than syntax.

Does my child need to bring a laptop?

Yes — every child brings their own laptop, and it needs a working USB port, because that is how the micro:bit and Arduino boards connect. Any reasonably recent Windows or Mac laptop is fine. Siblings attending together may share one laptop between them. Please send a charger too, along with a water bottle and a notebook.

Do they keep the kit?

The camp kits stay at the venue and are reused across cohorts — that's what keeps the fee where it is. A take-home kit can be bought separately from TechWiz if your child wants to keep building at home. Ask us for the current price.

What is the fee?

Fees differ between Minna and Abuja, and there's a reduced rate for siblings registering together. The exact figure is on the registration form, and we confirm it in writing before any payment.

What happens on the last day?

Demo Day. Parents are invited. Every child presents the thing they built and answers questions about it — for many of them it's the first time they've explained their own work to adults.

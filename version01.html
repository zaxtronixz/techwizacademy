What it feels like

The moment it moves without being pushed.

The robot rolls towards a wall. A sensor she wired notices. A line of code she wrote decides. It stops, turns, and goes around — and the room goes loud. That is the moment we build the whole week around. Everything before it is preparation, and everything after it is a child who now believes technology is something she makes, not something she buys.

The week, day by day

From “what is AI?” to a working prototype, in one week.

Two tracks, one theme

Every child is stretched, none are lost.

Same theme, same Demo Day, different depth. Children are placed by age and by what they can already do.

Foundation · Ages 8–11

BBC micro:bit

Block coding, LED patterns, sound and motion sensors. They build a gadget that reacts to the world around it — and can explain how it works to their grandmother.

Advanced · Ages 12–16

Keyestudio & Arduino

Real wiring, real components, text-based code. They assemble a four-wheel mecanum robot car with ultrasonic sensors, program it to sense and steer for itself, and debug it when it doesn’t.

Demo Day is for you. Come and be pitched to.

On the last day your child stands in front of the room with sixty seconds, a prototype, and a problem they chose themselves. Most parents tell us this is the part they did not expect.
